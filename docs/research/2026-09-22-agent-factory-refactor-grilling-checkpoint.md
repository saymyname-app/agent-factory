2. **Scope.** Refactor the project-agnostic Software Factory and its contracts with project repositories. Do not refactor SayMyName product code without a demonstrated need.
3. **Migration posture.** Reconstruct the Intent & Design Workflow while the existing Delivery Workflow may continue consuming already-created Beads. A planned interruption of delivery is acceptable later when migration safety or efficiency requires it.
4. **Project agnosticism.** Keep universal safety principles, but adapt autonomy and gates to project state (greenfield/brownfield/maturity) and change class (risk, novelty, oracle quality, reversibility).
5. **Design dossier.** Build the target progressively from observed state, principles, target workflow, gaps, then migration slices.
6. **Architecture presumption.** Retain Gas City, Beads/Dolt and the Mayor unless evidence falsifies their fit. They are not sacred.
7. **Planning entry hypothesis.** Use `grill-with-docs` as the provisional normal entry and promote to Wayfinder when the work exceeds one healthy session. Benjamin remains doubtful; test this against a Wayfinder-first alternative on real simple and complex requests.
8. **Questions and unknowns.** Questions discovered and resolved inside one grilling session need no Bead. Multi-session planning decisions need durable work. Implementation surprises that change intent, scope, approved boundaries or acceptance return the affected slice to planning; local factual or tactical discoveries do not automatically become Decision Beads.

10. **Work decomposition.** Adapt `to-tickets` to create tracer-bullet/vertical-slice work with explicit dependency edges in Beads. Inspect and apply `setup-matt-pocock-skills` prerequisites where relevant.
11. **Planning belongs inside the Gas City topology.** Keep one Mayor as execution/co-ordination authority, but not as the only planning context. Model independent Planning Runs in Gas City, with on-demand interactive planning sessions and formula-driven specialist agents. Codex, Claude and the Cockpit are operator surfaces/adapters, not a second factory above Gas City.
12. **Human review of the Spec.** Do not require Benjamin to reread a faithful summary by default. Require targeted review only for a semantic delta, contradiction, unresolved choice, lost traceability or a newly introduced test seam.
13. **Matt-first Method Baseline.** Reproduce Matt Pocock's method and observable contracts before adapting them: exhaustive grilling, prototype detours for runnable unknowns, one canonical Spec, tracer-bullet tickets with explicit blocking edges, fresh implementation context per ticket, TDD and independent review. Do not copy Sandcastle's implementation topology, tracker choice or provider assumptions. Record every deviation and added control in the single Adaptation Register with its constraint or evidence, then validate it through prototypes, canaries and measurements.
14. **Adaptive decomposition approval.** Reproduce Matt's explicit human approval of ticket granularity and blocking edges during calibration and for new or risky work classes. Remove that gate only for a class qualified by comparable canaries with no material decomposition drift or rework; restore it when confidence signals regress.
15. **Project-scoped planning configuration.** Configure methodology at the project level inside the City: the project profile identifies its Work Graph, authoritative domain sources, participating rigs, and genuine rig-local extensions. Do not duplicate the complete planning configuration into every rig or impose one global profile on every project.
16. **Matt/Gas City integration seam.** Q11 requires Gas City orchestration and topology, not the built-in `planning-base` artifact contract. Keep `to-spec` and `to-tickets` faithful to Matt's method: a temporary canonical Spec becomes self-contained vertical-slice Beads with explicit dependency edges and a convoy. Join the Gas City Delivery Workflow at the convoy continuation boundary. Keep upstream `planning-base` unchanged for methodologies that genuinely use separate requirements and plan artifacts; do not fabricate those projections for the Matt path unless a later compatibility experiment proves a concrete need.
17. **Planning continuity follows scale.** A simple Planning Run may remain centered on one interactive session. A Wayfinding Run is centered instead on a durable Planning Graph: one Planning Map Bead plus non-executable Planning Decision Beads for research, grilling, prototypes, or prerequisite tasks. The charting session may close after creating the graph; one disposable session resolves each frontier decision, creates newly discovered decision children or dependency edges, records its resolution and linked artifacts, then closes its own Bead. Launch eligible AFK research automatically; instantiate HITL sessions on demand so Benjamin never has to copy ticket links by hand.
18. **Exactly one `to-tickets`.** The Planning Run owns the only decomposition pass. Before approval it holds a temporary Ticket Graph Draft; after the Q15 gate, its publish phase calls a deterministic Mayor-owned write door that atomically creates the directly executable Vertical Slice Beads, their approved dependency edges, Spec trace, and convoy. The write door validates but never interprets or re-slices the draft. The Mayor owns dispatch, retries, recovery, evidence, and closure, not Delivery Work Graph design. Any later split, merge, or dependency-topology change returns to the Planning Run; tactical work that still fits a ticket stays inside that ticket, and operational repair Beads remain separate from the approved delivery graph.
19. **Temporary planning artifacts.** Store the Spec as readable Markdown and the Ticket Graph Draft as structured data owned by the Planning Run, each with a stable identity and content digest. Reference them from the Planning Graph and published Vertical Slice Beads instead of storing their full contents in Beads or treating them as permanent Git documentation. Copy each slice's autonomous execution contract into its Bead, freeze both artifacts during delivery, and allow them to expire after the convoy closes; closed Beads retain provenance while code, tests, glossary entries, and ADRs hold durable truth.
20. **One authoritative graph, two Work Planes.** Keep Planning and Delivery Beads in the same authoritative Beads/Dolt graph, separated by mandatory positive routing metadata. Planning Map and Planning Decision Beads use `plane=planning` and are never routable; published Vertical Slice Beads use `plane=delivery` and become routable only after validation and convoy attachment. Mayor selectors must positively require eligible delivery-plane work rather than subtract planning types from a generic ready set. Do not create a second tracker or graph store merely to isolate planning.
21. **Wayfinding closure and Spec approval.** Apply the upstream Wayfinder resolution rules to each Planning Decision Bead without inventing stricter child-resolution criteria. Fog belongs to the Planning Map, not to one child: after resolving a child, the Wayfinder updates the map and graduates newly specifiable fog into new children. When this normal map process declares the route clear and no open children remain, launch `to-spec` automatically; do not add a second fog audit at the handoff. Ask Benjamin for explicit approval after synthesis; approval makes the same Spec an Approved Spec and closes the Planning Map Bead. If synthesis exposes a real undecided question, return that question to the Planning Graph. A small automatic Spec integrity check may be tried during calibration, but is an experiment, not a permanent prerequisite.
22. **Semantic surprise during delivery.** A worker raises a structured Planning Exception. The Mayor parks only the affected delivery subgraph, and the same Planning Run resolves the question through a non-executable Planning Decision Bead. Tactical clarification may resume the existing slice; a behavioral or topology change creates immutable revised planning artifacts and a validated patch to unfinished Delivery Beads through the same authoritative `to-tickets` mechanism. This is an experimental adaptation, not a Sandcastle feature or a second decomposition authority.
23. **Planning primitive allocation.** Use named sessions for human conversation, specialist agents for research/design/review, formulas for phase sequencing, orders for eligibility-driven triggers, deterministic code for validation and graph publication, and skills for agent method. This is a target hypothesis to prototype against Gas City, not a commitment to preserve current Factory wiring.
24. **Provider and model qualification.** Define each planning role's needed capability, then compare providers and models on simple and complex canaries. Optimize token use together with quality and elapsed time, rather than minimizing token count alone. Test whether provider switches preserve Planning Run identity and decisions. The current provider-failover policy is legacy evidence, not the target default.
25. **Planning write ownership.** Keep Matt's Wayfinder writing protocol: each session claims and resolves one child, writes its resolution there, then updates the shared Planning Map; domain terms and qualifying ADRs are written when resolved. Allow independent children and research to proceed in parallel. Add the smallest concurrency protection needed for shared map and artifact writes, then prototype it: Matt's skill defines write order but no lock or version check, and a documented concurrent run lost a map update and double-resolved a child ([upstream issue #476](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/issues/476)). For Spec and Ticket Graph Draft, keep one canonical writer at a time; do not infer this from legacy rig policy.
26. **Experiment retirement authority.** Register each added gate and material adaptation with its baseline, signal, cost, review point, and replacement/removal rule. A technical check may retire automatically under preapproved criteria. Retirement of a human approval over meaning, including Spec approval, requires Benjamin's decision on evidence.
27. **Legacy knowledge is an archive.** Design the target agent contexts, memories, rules, ADRs, and project documentation from the new method and decisions. Set the present corpus aside as read-only legacy reference, consulted when a concrete old problem recurs or an old solution needs recovery; do not bulk-migrate or audit every legacy statement. A target rule that resembles an old one still needs its own current justification.
28. **Provider-independent planning method.** The interviewer, researcher, synthesizer, and reviewer have the same job and acceptance standard regardless of whether Codex or Claude performs it. Keep only the startup/tool adaptations actually needed by each provider. Prototype interruption and resumption, checking that resolved decisions remain available without Benjamin repeating them. A Planning Run is the whole bounded design journey; one agent conversation is only a session within it.
29. **Operator-surface continuity.** Benjamin must resume the relevant Planning Run from Codex, Claude Code, or the Cockpit without copying the full conversation or repeating decisions, at low or zero extra model cost. Show the same conversation in multiple surfaces only if a native/read-only view can do so with zero model calls and little maintenance. Benjamin has observed Claude Code conversations appearing in Codex but not the reverse; verify that asymmetry in a prototype rather than assuming its mechanism or guaranteeing bidirectional transcript mirroring.
30. **High initial concurrency ceiling.** The machine's observed resource pressure is the practical cap; start with a deliberately high limit, test under realistic concurrent planning load, and let Benjamin adjust the limit if problems appear. Do not inherit legacy session caps by default. Measure token cost and outcome quality separately from machine load.
31. **Decision-session retention.** Once a Wayfinder child has recorded its resolution, close its interactive session. Retain it briefly for diagnosis at first, then experiment with reducing the retention period to zero; the Planning Graph and linked evidence carry continuity.
32. **One Adaptation Register.** Use one record for deviations from Matt's Method Baseline, added gates, and experiments. Each entry carries its reason, evidence, cost, review point, and retain/replace/remove outcome. Views may filter it, but no second register duplicates entries.
33. **Outcome measures by work class.** Compare lost decisions, escaped defects, human rework, and maintainability first; then elapsed time and tokens per accepted outcome. Compare comparable simple and complex classes rather than hiding regressions in a single aggregate. The old Factory is comparison data, not the target design authority.
34. **Direct cutover, fix forward.** Once the new planning path is operable, make it the only path for new work. Never return new work to the old planning path; correct defects in the new one and consult archived legacy only for evidence. Already-started Delivery Workflow items may finish under Q3. No staged project-by-project fallback or dual planning paths are intended.
35. **Separate Factory and Project Knowledge.** Build the target's small versioned knowledge base from validated decisions: reusable Factory methods and rules remain project-agnostic and shareable; each project owns its domain language, product/architecture decisions, and repository rules. The Project Planning Profile points to the project's authoritative sources without copying them into the Factory or every rig. Legacy knowledge stays archived.
36. **Pause and abandon.** Pausing a Planning Run retains its map, resolved decisions, and linked evidence while releasing active agent sessions; resumption refreshes only facts that may have changed. Abandonment closes the run without publishing Delivery Work Graph items.
37. **Portable per-operator Factory.** Distribute the reusable Factory method so each operator can install an independent City with their own projects and access. A shared multi-user service is a separate future decision, not the initial target. Deliver a concise README setup and getting-started tutorial for a new operator, completed against the implemented target rather than copied from legacy instructions.
38. **Project adaptation boundary.** Keep a small common set of safety principles for people and data. A Project Planning Profile selects its own authoritative sources, checks, risk thresholds, and autonomy within those principles; project instructions cannot silently disable the common boundary.
39. **Outcome acceptance.** The project owner validates delivered behavior for the first outcomes and for new or risky change classes. For proven classes, predefined evidence may replace that human approval. Register this gate and its retirement conditions in the Adaptation Register; do not hardcode Benjamin as the owner of every project.
40. **New-path outage.** After direct cutover, suspend only affected Planning Runs and repair the new workflow. Independent Delivery Workflow items continue. A manual repair or decision still uses the new Planning Graph and Spec contract; the legacy planning path is not reactivated.
41. **Initial host scope.** Benjamin has no professional cross-platform support requirement. His Windows machine running Ubuntu under WSL is enough for the initial Factory; other hosts are optional later and require real verification before claims of support. Sharing with friends or as a public GitHub portfolio is secondary.
42. **Git distribution only.** Version the Factory in Git and include a small README setup/getting-started tutorial. Each adopter owns their own updates. Do not design managed upgrades, release channels, or support for others as initial requirements.
43. **Project access boundary.** An agent working on one project cannot read another project's files by default. Cross-project work must explicitly name its participating projects. Gas City's per-rig work organization is not proof of filesystem permission isolation; verify the actual access boundary in a prototype.
44. **Zero-token ordinary monitoring.** Normal checks use deterministic observations and no model calls while state is unchanged. Wake an agent only for a new fact needing judgment or repair. Added controls remain in the Adaptation Register for value/cost review.
45. **No pre-cutover simple/complex trial gate.** The old planning path is already stopped and will not be restarted. Once the new path exists, use it directly on the next real requests, repairing it forward. Do not make a simple-plus-complex pilot campaign a prerequisite to activation. Prototypes of uncertain mechanics and normal implementation tests can still inform the design, and the first live outcomes supply canary evidence afterward.
46. **Model cost ceiling.** Route difficult planning/synthesis/review to the most capable models that fit the budget, and simpler bounded work to cheaper models, measuring quality and elapsed time as well as tokens. Never use Astra or Fable for this Factory under the current policy; medium is the maximum reasoning effort, with lower effort where adequate. The method should work without premium models.
47. **TDD author continuity.** One implementer writes a failing public-behavior test and the minimal implementation in small red/green cycles within a fresh ticket session. Do not require a separate test-author agent by default; a split is a measurable adaptation only when a real class of work justifies its extra context handoff. Test seams come from the approved contract, not a fresh human approval for each cycle.
48. **Cost-aware independent code review.** Run deterministic checks on every ticket. Initially use one independent reviewer per ticket to examine both fidelity to the ticket/Spec and non-automated design quality; this consolidates Matt's two independent review axes and is a registered, measured adaptation. After comparable live evidence shows low yield for a repeatable low-risk class, permit that class to skip model review under predefined criteria, while sampling skipped tickets and tracking escaped defects/rework. Keep new, sensitive, weak-oracle, cross-ticket, and broad changes review-eligible. Do not duplicate full per-ticket reviews at the whole-feature boundary; use integration evidence and targeted review of interactions instead. Record cost, catches, and retirement/restoration rules in the Adaptation Register.
49. **Delivered versus accepted.** Report a result as delivered when code and required technical evidence are complete. Report it as accepted only after the project owner confirms the behavior, or predefined evidence suffices for a proven work class. Keep these states visible and distinct.
50. **Human decision for sensitive effects.** Require an explicit human decision before widening access to personal data, unrecoverable destruction, real sending or publication, spending, or production action. A project may preauthorize bounded reversible actions in an isolated environment within the shared safety principles.
51. **Whole-outcome design review.** For a multi-ticket outcome, review the exact assembled diff once after ticket integration to catch emergent architecture/design problems and cross-ticket Spec drift that per-ticket reviews and GitHub checks may miss. Use one independent reviewer at no more than medium effort, focused on unique whole-outcome issues rather than repeating ticket-level comments; no duplicate final model review for a single-ticket outcome. Register this added gate with its unique findings, token cost, and retain/replace/remove criteria. Matt's `code-review` supports an arbitrary fixed-point diff but his `implement` invokes it per ticket; the final review is our deliberate addition. Build the assembled diff from ticket/PR provenance if a shared integration branch contains unrelated changes.
52. **Integration before outcome acceptance.** After required verification, reversible ticket code may merge into the project's configured integration branch before the project owner accepts the delivered behavior. Acceptance remains a separate state; this does not authorize production, external publication, or sensitive effects. The legacy Factory's green-CI merge mechanism is evidence to inspect and potentially reuse by fresh justification, not a target policy by default.
53. **Bounded repair.** Keep technical test/review/CI repair within the affected ticket for a bounded attempt budget, preserving failure evidence while unrelated tickets continue. A semantic or topology change returns through the Q22 Planning Exception. Tune the exact budget from observed failure/cost data rather than inventing a universal count now.
54. **Dependency readiness.** A dependent delivery ticket becomes eligible only after its prerequisite's required tests/review have passed and its code is integrated into the project's configured working branch. Unrelated eligible tickets may proceed in parallel. This is a target interpretation of `to-tickets` blocker edges and Q52, not an upstream skill command.
55. **Cross-repository outcome evidence.** For an outcome spanning repositories, assemble the exact participating frontend/backend or other component revisions in an isolated environment and verify end-to-end behavior before declaring Delivered Outcome. Per-repository green checks alone do not establish interoperability; this does not authorize production exposure.
56. **Acceptance rejection after integration.** If the project owner rejects a Delivered Outcome, repair a mismatch with the Approved Spec as targeted delivery work; a changed desired behavior returns to planning; an actively dangerous integrated change is safely removed or neutralized. Do not roll back the entire outcome or revive the legacy planning path by default.

## Durable terminology

Use `CONTEXT.md` as the canonical glossary. In particular:

- **Software Factory** is the entire socio-technical system.
- **Gas City** is the orchestration platform/runtime used by the Factory.
- **City** is one configured Gas City workspace.
- **Mayor** is the unique orchestration authority, not the whole Factory and not necessarily the only human-facing agent.
- **Planning Run** is a bounded, independently resumable instance of the Intent & Design Workflow.
- **Planning Graph** is the non-executable decision graph that anchors a multi-session Wayfinding Run.
- **Planning Artifact** is a temporary file artifact with stable identity and content digest; it is referenced by Beads but is neither a Bead nor permanent documentation.
- **Ticket Graph Draft** is the temporary pre-publication representation reviewed by `to-tickets`; it is not executable work.
- **Method Baseline** is the faithful reference workflow used before deliberate adaptation.
- **Adaptation Register** combines deviations, added controls, and experiments in one evidence-backed record.
- **Factory Knowledge** is reusable method and rules; **Project Knowledge** is one project's domain and repository context.
- **Project Planning Profile** binds one project's planning methodology to its authoritative context, Work Graph, and participating rigs.
- **Work Plane** is the mandatory positive routing classification that makes Planning Beads non-routable and validated Delivery Beads eligible for execution.
- **Planning Exception** is a delivery signal that returns an approved-intent, behavior, or work-topology question to the Planning Run.
- **Delivered Outcome** has completed technical implementation and required verification; **Accepted Outcome** has passed the project owner's behavior check or its class's predefined acceptance evidence.
- **Cockpit** is an operator interface; it must not become a second orchestrator.
